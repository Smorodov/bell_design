# Chinese Bell Literature

Maintained bibliography for the Chinese-bell mechanical/acoustic work in BellOptimizer.

## Primary scientific references

1. **M. Jing**, “A theoretical study of the vibration and acoustics of ancient Chinese bells,” *The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*, 114(3), 1622–1628 (2003). DOI: `10.1121/1.1600727`.
   - Theoretical treatment of ancient Chinese two-tone bells, special strike positions, minor/major-third pitch relationship, lens-shaped cross section, and attenuation.
   - PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14514215/

2. **Tong Chen and Darui Zheng**, “Acoustical Properties of Chime Bells,” *Chinese Journal of Acoustics*, 4(1), 1–10 (1985). DOI: `10.15949/j.cnki.0217-9776.1985.01.001`.
   - Experimental measurements of vibrational modes and spectra. The abstract explicitly reports two tones dependent on strike position and similar modes with different nodal-line positions.
   - Publisher: https://www.sciengine.com/doi/10.15949/j.cnki.0217-9776.1985.01.001

3. **Thomas D. Rossing, D. Scott Hampton, Bernard E. Richardson, H. John Sathoff, and Andre Lehr**, “Vibrational modes of Chinese two-tone bells,” *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* (1987).
   - Foundational experimental modal study using holographic interferometry and sound-field scanning. It reports paired modes, complementary node/antinode placement at the spine, and different notes at different strike positions.
   - A searchable copy is available through the acoustical literature archive; the project uses this reference for the modal-pair mechanism rather than for numerical parameter calibration.

4. **Jianming Tsai, Zhiqing Jiang, and Thomas D. Rossing**, “Vibrational Modes of a Modern Chinese Two-Tone Bell,” *Acoustics Australia*, 20(1), 17–19 (1992).
   - Experimental study of an almond-shaped modern Chinese bell. The abstract describes mode pairs and complementary node/antinode placement at the xian/spine.
   - Accessible copy: https://acoustics.asn.au/journal/Vol20No1.pdf

5. **Wang Dajun, Chen Jian, and Wang Huijun**, “The Feature of the Dual Tones of Chinese Music Bells,” *Mechanics in Engineering*, 25(4) (2003). DOI: `10.6052/1000-0992-2002-411`.
   - Vibration-theory and FEM treatment of the Chinese dual-tone phenomenon.
   - Publisher: https://pubs.cstam.org.cn/article/doi/10.6052/1000-0992-2002-411?viewType=HTML

## Modern reconstruction / experimental context

6. **Kin Sum Li**, “Digital and physical re-creation of ancient Chinese bells: new understandings and discoveries,” *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*, 12, 48 (2025). DOI: `10.1057/s41599-024-04133-8`.
   - Recent reconstruction study documenting digital scanning, physical reproduction, wooden-mallet excitation and A/B strike positions. Useful for practical reconstruction context; it does not by itself calibrate the production contact model.
   - Publisher: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-04133-8

7. **“A ‘set’ of ancient bronze bells excavated in Changsha, Hunan Province, China,”** *npj Heritage Science*.
   - Recent archaeological/acoustic context and bibliography connecting modern Chinese-bell research with earlier modal and acoustic studies.
   - Publisher: https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-024-01377-0

8. **Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art**, “In Sync: Ancient Chinese Bronze Bells at the Smithsonian.”
   - Accessible historical/acoustic overview of lens-shaped bells and strike positions.
   - https://music.si.edu/story/sync-ancient-chinese-bronze-bells-smithsonian

## General bell impact/contact reference

9. **Carvalho et al.**, “Physical modelling techniques for the dynamical characterization and sound synthesis of historical bells,” *npj Heritage Science* 9, 157 (2021). DOI: `10.1186/s40494-021-00620-2`.
   - General bell impact/contact modelling reference. Its contact parameters are not transferred directly to the Chinese wooden-mallet problem.
   - Publisher: https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-021-00620-2

## Background reference

10. **N. H. Fletcher and T. D. Rossing**, *The Physics of Musical Instruments*, 2nd ed., Springer.
   - General background on musical-instrument vibration, bell modes, degeneracy and symmetry breaking. Project-specific Chinese-bell claims prefer the primary sources above.

## Source-use policy

- Primary experimental/theoretical papers are preferred for physical claims.
- General bell literature is used for mathematical context, not to manufacture Chinese-bell parameters.
- Recent reconstruction studies provide practical context but do not automatically establish historical authenticity.
- Generic contact-model parameters are not promoted to Chinese production parameters without independent evidence.
